# Speaker Script — Zero Day CPT Presentation

## General Tips
- Do not read the slides. The slides are visuals. YOU tell the story.
- Pause after big statements. Let them breathe.
- Make eye contact. Do not stare at the screen.
- Speak like you are explaining to a friend, not reading an essay.
- Target: 8-9 minutes (added 3 new slides).

---

## Slide 1 — Title (5 seconds)
Nothing to say here. Just let them see the title. Maybe nod or smile. Move on.

---

## Slide 2 — The Hook (20 seconds)
Read the two questions slowly. Pause between them.

"What if your entire town was built on a lie? What if the air you breathed was slowly killing you and nobody in power cared?"

Then say: "That is Drake, West Virginia. The setting of Zero Day. A town that has been drained of its life by greed and industry."

Optional: Read the quote on the slide if you want. Or just say it in your own words.

---

## Slide 3 — Book Summary (30 seconds)
This is your summary slide. Keep it moving.

"A family gets murdered in Drake, West Virginia. It is a small coal-mining town that is basically falling apart. John Puller is an Army CID agent — that is like the Army's own detective unit. He gets sent to investigate. He teams up with Sam Cole, a local detective who knows the town."

"They start digging. They find out the mining company has been poisoning the water and air for years. People have been getting sick. People have died. And anyone who tried to speak up kept disappearing."

Say this last line with weight: "It is basically a story about greed, silence, and one soldier who refuses to look away."

---

## Slide 4 — The Investigation (20 seconds)
Walk through the four steps. Point at each one.

"Step one: A family is murdered. Brutal crime scene. Step two: Puller arrives from the Army. Step three: He meets Sam Cole, the local detective who has her own reasons for helping. Step four: They start pulling the thread and the whole conspiracy starts coming apart."

---

## Slide 5 — What They Find (25 seconds)
"It was not just one crime. It was an entire system built on silence."

Then walk through the three boxes:
- "The company — Coaling Energy — has been dumping toxic waste into the water for years. They knew."
- "The cover-up — government officials took payouts. Environmental reports got buried."
- "The cost — people got cancer. Kids got sick. Graves kept filling up."

---

## Slide 6 — Theme 1: Environment (25 seconds)
"Drake is not just a dying town. It was killed. Decades of coal mining destroyed everything — the land, the water, the air."

"This is what happens when companies treat a place like something to use up and throw away."

Then connect it: "This is happening right now. Fossil fuels, ozone depletion, industrial waste — communities today are living this. The book just puts a face to it."

---

## Slide 7 — Theme 2: Corruption (25 seconds)
"The people in charge did not forget about the poisoning. They chose to ignore it. Because stopping it would cost money."

"Anyone who tried to expose the truth got killed. Not threatened. Killed."

"This is how corruption works in dictatorships and regimes right now. The powerful silence the people who ask questions. The only difference in Drake is it happened in America."

---

## Slide 8 — Theme 3: Trauma & Trust (25 seconds)
"Puller is a soldier. He has seen combat. But Drake is different. The enemy is not on a battlefield — it is a corporation. A system. People in suits."

"The hardest part is trust. Who can he believe in a town where everyone has been silenced?"

Say this with some weight: "Modern warfare is not just about whose military is stronger. The most dangerous fights are against anonymous enemies. That is what Puller is up against."

---

## Slide 9 — Key Characters (20 seconds)
"John Puller is disciplined, relentless, and loyal. He does not stop. Even when the conspiracy goes higher than he expected."

"Sam Cole grew up in Drake. She left. She came back. She lost people to the same sickness killing the town. For her, this is personal."

"They are an unlikely pair. Together they are dangerous to the people who want the truth buried."

---

## Slide 10 — Why This Matters Now (20 seconds)
"This book is not just a story from 2011. It is still relevant."

Walk through the three boxes quickly:
- "Energy and environment debates are happening right now."
- "Toxic dumping and corporate climate damage are not fiction."
- "And the question of who to trust when the powerful are causing the harm — that matters more than ever."

---

## Slide 11 — Memorable Moments (25 seconds)
Pick 2-3 of these to talk about. Do not read all four.

"The crime scene is brutal. It is clear this was a message, not random."

"The brother of the murdered family tries to warn Puller before he dies. That moment sets everything in motion."

"Sam tells Puller why she really came back. That is when the case becomes personal."

Optional: Read the quote. "Things were not always as they seemed. Nobody in Drake was who they appeared to be."

---

## Slide 12 — English Skills (20 seconds)
"This book is also useful for learning real English skills."

Point at the cards:
- "Setting as character — Drake is basically its own character. The town shapes everything."
- "Types of conflict — Man vs. Society when Puller fights the system. Man vs. Self when he figures out who to trust."
- "Voice and tone — Puller talks in a very specific military way. Short sentences. Tactical language."
- "Teachers could use this book for class discussions about corporate ethics or for analyzing atmosphere in writing."

---

## Slide 13 — Persuasive Techniques (20 seconds)
"Here are the persuasive techniques I used in this presentation and why."

Walk through them quickly:
- "Rhetorical questions in the hook — they pull you in without needing an answer."
- "Pathos — the emotional stuff. Kids getting sick. Graves filling up. That makes you feel why it matters."
- "Logos — the logical evidence. Company dumping toxins. Government cover-ups."
- "Ethos — using direct quotes from the book so you are hearing it from the source."
- "Imagery — descriptive language that makes Drake feel real. Like the air that felt heavy, like it was pushing you into the ground."

---

## Slide 14 — Media Component (20 seconds)
"If I was marketing this book to new readers, here is what I would do."

Pick 2-3 concepts:
- "Redesigned book cover — dark mountains, thick fog, one light from a mine. Focus on the creepy atmosphere instead of the military angle."
- "Movie poster — a foggy town at night, one figure walking into darkness. Tagline: 'The truth is buried deep.'"
- "Instagram and TikTok — dark aesthetic posts with intense quotes. A 30-second book trailer with quick cuts and dark music."
- "Character artwork — stylized art of Puller and Sam in Drake. People connect with faces."

---

## Slide 15 — Why Read It (20 seconds)
"Zero Day is not just a thriller. It is a mirror."

"It reads fast. Baldacci knows how to keep you turning pages. But underneath the action, the book is asking real questions: What happens when the people protecting you are the ones hurting you? How far would you go to expose the truth?"

Say this with confidence: "That is basically what Zero Day is. A thriller that does not let you look away from the real stuff."

---

## Slide 16 — Closing (10 seconds)
Just read the closing line. Pause. Make eye contact.

"Truth is worth fighting for. Even when it is buried. Even when it is dangerous. Even when nobody else will."

Then: "Thank you. Any questions?"

- **West Virginia** — "Vur-JIN-ee-uh"
- **Puller** — "PULL-er" (like pulling something)
- **CID** — Just say the letters: "C-I-D"
- **Coaling Energy** — "COAL-ing"
