**SLIDE 2 — The Hook**

  

**Tagline:** The Hook

  

**Statement:** What if your entire town was built on a lie? / What if the air you breathed was slowly killing you and the people who knew the truth were being silenced one by one?

  

**Body:** That is Drake, West Virginia. The setting of Zero Day, a town that has been drained of its life by greed and industry, where the truth is buried just as deep as the coal mines.

  

**Quote:** "The place was tired. Dying. Maybe already dead." — David Baldacci, Zero Day, p. 32

  

---

  

**SLIDE 3 — About the Book**

  

**Tagline:** About the Book

  

**Body:** Zero Day follows John Puller, a U.S. Army CID special agent, who is sent to investigate the brutal murder of an entire family in a crumbling coal-mining town in West Virginia.

  

**Flow Diagram:**

- Step 1: Family murdered — Drake, WV

- Step 2: Puller arrives — Army CID investigation

- Step 3: Meets Sam Cole — Local detective

- Step 4: Uncovers conspiracy — Gov corruption



*CID agent meaning Criminal Investigation Department

---

  

**SLIDE 4 — Themes**

  

**Tagline:** Why This Book Matters

  

**Card 1 — Environmental Destruction:** Decades of coal mining have ruined the land, the air and the people of Drake. Toxic dust, sulfur dioxide and dead grass everywhere. / Real world: fossil fuels and ozone gases today

  

**Card 2 — Corruption & Power:** The people in charge put profit over human lives. Anyone who tries to expose the truth gets killed. / Government and powerful corporations censorship in Dictatorships, Monarchies and Regimes

  

**Card 3 — Trauma & Trust:** Puller is a soldier that is fighting a big cooperation that is also invisible and anonymous . / Today's warfare is not necessarily about how strong a military is but how anonymous a military is

**SLIDE 5 — Memorable Moments**

  

**Tagline:** Memorable Moments

  

**Statement:** One of the most haunting scenes is when Puller visits the crime scene and toys were still scattered everywhere from the family that was just wiped out

  

**Body:** Another moment: Puller visits his brother Robert in a military prison. Their conversation is full of guild and regret
  

**Quote:** "Docs? Like your lawyers, not much they can do." — Robert Puller to John, Zero Day, p. 15

  

---

  

**SLIDE 6 — Characters**

  

**Tagline:** Key Characters

  

**Card 1 — John Puller (Army CID Agent):**

- Hyper-efficient, tactical mind

- Sits with his back to the wall

- Haunted by Iraq and Afghanistan

- "Does not believe in coincidences"

- Accent: "Cause and effect"

  

**Card 2 — Sam Cole (Local Detective):**

- Tough, no-nonsense attitude

- Deep connection to Drake

- Represents the emotional side

- Trusts Puller despite her instincts

- Accent: "Two worlds, one goal"

**SLIDE 7 — Why Now**

  

**Tagline:** Why This Book Matters Now — Ethos + Imagery

  

**Body:** Zero Day is basically perfect for right now because it deals with stuff we see on the news every day:

  

**Box 1 — Energy Crisis:** Fossil fuels vs. renewables — the human cost of keeping the lights on

  

**Box 2 — Climate Change:** Environmental damage that hits poor communities the hardest

  

**Box 3 — Trust in Government:** People do not trust institutions anymore. Doom scroll for five minutes right now and you will see why.

**Body 2:** Reading this book, you can almost feel the grit in your lungs. Baldacci does not just tell you the town is dying — he shows you the cracked asphalt, the stunted grass, the rusted trucks falling apart in driveways.

**SLIDE 8 — English Skills**

  

**Tagline:** Connection to English Class

  

**Body:** This book is not just a page-turner. It teaches you real English skills:

  

**Flow Diagram:**

- Setting as Character → The town of Drake IS its own character

- Voice & Tone → Puller talks in a military-specific way

- Conflict Types → Man vs. Society, Man vs. Self

- Theme Development → Environmental justice, corruption, trauma

  

**Body 2:** It also works great for practicing atmosphere analysis because the descriptions of Drake are so vivid.

  

---

  

**SLIDE 9 — Why Read It**

  

**Tagline:** So Why Should You Read Zero Day?

  

**Statement:** Because it makes you think about the air you breathe, the institutions you trust, and the people who fight for the truth even when the odds are stacked against them.

  

**Body:** It is fast-paced, suspenseful, and genuinely hard to put down. If you like Lee Child, Tom Clancy, or true crime documentaries — this book is for you.

  

**Comparison — Reads Like:** Jack Reacher meets your local news

  

**Comparison — Feels Like:** A thriller show you binge on the weekend

  

**Quote:** "In the real world it often took a lot longer, and sometimes you never did get to the truth." — John Puller, Zero Day, p. 25
