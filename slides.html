The Hook
What if your entire town was built on a lie?

What if the air you breathed was slowly killing you
and nobody in power cared?
"The air in Drake didn't just smell bad; it felt heavy, like it was trying to push you into the ground." — David Baldacci, Zero Day, p. 42
The Whole Story
A family is murdered in a dying coal-mining town. An Army investigator and a local detective uncover a conspiracy that goes all the way up — and someone is killing to keep it buried.
John Puller is an Army CID agent — that is the Army's own detective unit. He gets sent to Drake, West Virginia to investigate the brutal murder of an entire family. He teams up with Sam Cole, a local detective who has lived in Drake her whole life.

They keep digging. They find out the mining company has been poisoning the water and the air for years. People have been getting sick. People have died. And anyone who tried to speak up kept disappearing. The conspiracy goes all the way up — government officials, company executives, cover-ups that go back decades.

It is basically a story about greed, silence, and one soldier who refuses to look away.

Setting — Drake, West Virginia
Drake is not just a backdrop. It is basically its own character in this book.

Cookie-cutter houses. Rusted trucks. Dead grass everywhere. Coal dust on every surface. Sulfur dioxide in the air. The whole place feels like it is falling apart — because it is.

Baldacci describes a town that is broke and sick. Everyone is breathing in toxic coal dust. The mines are still running but the people are not getting anything from it. The money goes somewhere else.

The setting is the most important thing to show in a campaign because it creates a dark mood that explains why the mystery is so dangerous and real.
"The place was tired. Dying. Maybe already dead." — David Baldacci, Zero Day, p. 32
Theme 1 — Environmental Destruction
Drake is not just a dying town. It was killed.

Decades of coal mining have destroyed the land. The grass is dead. The water is toxic. Sulfur dioxide in the air. Coal dust on every surface. Baldacci describes the town like a body that stopped fighting.

This is not exaggeration. This is what happens when companies treat a place like a resource to use up and throw away. The people who live there are left behind with the mess — and the cancer.

Real world: This is happening right now. Fossil fuel emissions, ozone depletion, and industrial waste are still poisoning communities today. The book just puts a name and a face to it.

For students: You do not have to live in a coal town to understand this. Any time someone in power ignores a problem because fixing it costs too much — that is the same thing. It could be a school, a city, a country. The pattern is the same.
Theme 2 — Corruption & Power
The people in charge in Drake did not forget about the poisoning. They chose to ignore it. Because stopping it would cost money. And money is what mattered more than lives.

Anyone who tried to expose the truth got killed. Not threatened. Not fired. Killed. That is how serious this cover-up was.

The book shows how power works when nobody is watching. Company executives, local government, maybe even federal regulators — all of them choosing profit over people.

Real world: This is how corruption works in dictatorships, monarchies, and regimes right now. The powerful silence the people who ask questions. The only difference in Drake is it happened in America.

For students: You see smaller versions of this all the time. When a school ignores bullying because dealing with it is inconvenient. When a company lies about its products. When someone with power protects someone else with power. It is the same pattern — just a different scale.
Theme 3 — Trauma & Trust
Puller is a soldier. He has seen combat. He knows what violence looks like. But Drake is different. This is not a battlefield with a clear enemy. The enemy here is invisible — a corporation, a system, people in suits.

And the hardest part for Puller is trust. Who can he believe in a town where everyone has been silenced? Who is scared? Who is lying? Who is next?

Sam Cole has her own trauma. She left Drake and came back. She lost people to the same sickness killing the town. For her this is personal.

Real world: Modern warfare is not just about whose military is stronger. The most dangerous fights are the ones against anonymous enemies — systems, networks, people you never see coming. That is what Puller is up against.

For students: Everyone has things from their past that make it hard to trust people. Maybe you got hurt before. Maybe someone let you down. Puller and Sam both carry that. But they still choose to work together. That is the whole point — you do not have to be perfect or healed to do something that matters.
Key Characters
John Puller
U.S. Army CID Agent
Disciplined Relentless Analytical Loyal
A soldier and an investigator. He does not stop. Even when the conspiracy goes higher than he expected. He fights for people who cannot fight for themselves. His hyper-efficiency and tactical intelligence make him seem like a machine that will not stop until he finds the truth.
Sam Cole
Local Detective — Drake, WV
Brave Haunted Honest Stubborn
She grew up in Drake. She left. She came back. She knows the town and the people. For her, this case is about more than justice — it is about home. She represents the emotional side of the story and shows how the crime affects the local community.
They are an unlikely pair. Together they are dangerous to the people who want the truth buried.
Memorable Moments & Lessons

The Crime Scene
Puller arrives at the murdered family's house. The details are brutal. It is clear this was not random — it was a message. Lesson: Small crimes often hide much bigger problems.
2
Howard Reed's Condition
Seeing how coal mining has physically broken people like Howard Reed shows the victims are not just the ones who were murdered — it is the whole town. Lesson: Industrial greed has a permanent human cost.
3
Puller Realizes the Scale
When Puller understands the conspiracy goes all the way up, a local murder becomes a massive threat. Lesson: You cannot always judge a situation by how it looks on the surface.
4
Puller and Sam Learning to Trust
Two people from different worlds figuring out how to work together while everything falls apart. Lesson: People can overcome their differences for a common goal — like finding the truth.
English Skills You Can Learn From This Book
Setting as Character
Drake is not just where the story happens. It is basically its own character. The town is dying and that shapes everything — the mood, the choices people make, the way the mystery unfolds.
Types of Conflict
Man vs. Society: Puller fighting a corrupt system designed to protect powerful people.
Man vs. Self: Puller figuring out who to trust in a town built on secrets.
Voice & Tone
Puller talks in a very specific military way that is different from how a regular person would explain things. Baldacci uses voice to make each character sound distinct.
How Teachers Could Use This
Class discussions about environmental justice. Analyzing atmosphere in writing — Drake's descriptions are vivid. Complements other course texts about outsider perspectives and corrupt systems.
Persuasive Techniques Used
Rhetorical Questions
"What if your entire town was built on a lie?" — makes the audience think before you give them the answer.
Pathos (Emotion)
The human cost — people getting cancer, kids getting sick, graves filling up. Makes you feel something, not just think.
Logos (Logic)
Real-world connections to fossil fuels, climate damage, and government distrust. Facts that back up the book's themes.
Ethos (Authority)
Direct quotes from the book with MLA citations. Shows the evidence comes from the source, not just my opinion.
Imagery
"The air felt heavy, like it was trying to push you into the ground." — descriptive language that makes Drake feel real.
Anecdote
Specific scenes from the book — the crime scene, Howard Reed, Puller and Sam — used as stories to support each point.

It is a mirror.

How far would you go to expose the truth?
What does it cost to stand up when everyone else stays silent?
